Before you pack

This guide intentionally makes packing slower.

That is a feature, not a problem.

A shoebox can be packed quickly, but this guide helps a family pause long enough to connect each item with Scripture, prayer, gratitude, generosity, and the real child who may receive the gift.

You do not have to complete everything in one sitting. Adapt the questions for your child's age, attention span, and spiritual maturity.

One Family Night

Pack the entire box together, moving through the twelve experiences as a family devotional.

Twelve Moments

Complete one short experience at a time, then add that item to the box.

Shopping + Packing

Use the categories and item ideas while choosing OCC-compliant gifts, then return to the guide when placing your selections into the box.

Official rules check

Verify before you close the box.

Official OCC Recommendations

  • Make the gift fun, full, and personalized.
  • Include one larger wow item.
  • Add other toys, school supplies, clothing/accessories, crafts/activities, and personal care items.
  • Include a personal note and photo when appropriate.
  • Pray for the child, family, local church or pastor, and community.

Last verified: 2026-09-25.

Do Not Include

  • soap
  • food, candy, or gum
  • toothpaste
  • used or damaged items
  • scary or war-related items
  • wearable traditional camouflage
  • seeds or organic matter
  • liquids or lotions
  • medication or vitamins
  • money
  • breakable items
  • matches, fire starters, or aerosols
  • knife, saw, or razor blades
  • alcohol wipes

Rules can change. Check the official OCC gift suggestions and item search every year before final packing.

01

Pack A Wow Item

Created for Joy

Because God made children with delight and dignity, we can give something that says, "You are worth celebrating."

Pack Packing Ideas

These are suggestions, not a required or complete list. Choose OCC-compliant items that fit your child, budget, and shoebox.

  • stuffed animal
  • doll
  • deflated soccer ball with pump

Talk Talk About It

Start with the item that will probably catch the child's eye first. A good wow item is not just big; it is personal, cheerful, and chosen with care. God is not careless with people. Scripture begins with a Creator who calls His work good and who makes people in His image. When we choose something beautiful or fun, we are practicing a small kind of attentiveness: another child is not an idea or a project, but a person who can laugh, play, and receive a gift with joy.

For younger children:Ask your child to hold the item and imagine handing it to another child. What might make that child smile?

Go deeper:Talk about the difference between giving leftovers and giving with honor. What does a thoughtful gift communicate about the giver and the receiver?

Read Read God's Word

So God created man in his own image, … And God saw everything that he had made, and behold, it was very good.

Genesis 1:27, 31 ESVThis passage grounds human dignity in God's creation, not in what a person owns.

“Let the children come to me; do not hinder them, for to such belongs the kingdom of God. "

Mark 10:14 ESVJesus welcomes children as real participants in God's kingdom.

Ask Ask Your Child

  1. What makes this item feel joyful?
  2. How can a gift show honor without pretending we know everything about the child receiving it?
  3. What does it mean that every child is made in God's image?

Connect Think About the Child Receiving This

We do not know the child who may open this gift, but God does. Choose this item as though you were preparing it for someone whose life matters deeply to Him.

Pray Pray Together

Creator God, thank You for making every child in Your image. Please let this gift bring joy, and help the child who receives it know that they are seen and loved by You.

02

Pack Play

Good Gifts

Good gifts can remind us that our Father is generous and kind.

Pack Packing Ideas

These are suggestions, not a required or complete list. Choose OCC-compliant items that fit your child, budget, and shoebox.

  • jump rope
  • yo-yo
  • small toy cars or figures

Talk Talk About It

Play is not wasted time. Children learn, rest, imagine, and make friends through play. When we pack a simple toy, we are not pretending it solves every problem; we are saying that joy matters. Jesus taught that earthly parents know how to give good gifts, and that our heavenly Father is even more generous. A toy can begin a conversation about God's kindness and the way Christians give because we have first received from Him.

For younger children:Try the toy for a moment, then talk about how it feels to share something fun.

Go deeper:Discuss how generosity changes when we see our possessions as gifts from God rather than things we must protect at all costs.

Read Read God's Word

And the streets of the city shall be full of boys and girls playing in its streets.

Zechariah 8:5In Zechariah's picture of restored community, children playing safely is a sign of blessing and peace.

If you then, who are evil, know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more will your Father who is in heaven give good things to those who ask him!

Matthew 7:11Jesus points from ordinary family giving to the greater goodness of the Father.

Ask Ask Your Child

  1. What is a gift you remember because someone chose it especially for you?
  2. How does receiving from God help us give to others?
  3. What is the difference between showing off and sharing joy?

Connect Think About the Child Receiving This

The child receiving this may play alone, with siblings, or with friends. Pray that this small toy becomes part of real moments of gladness.

Pray Pray Together

Father, You give good gifts. Make our hearts generous, and let this toy bring laughter and friendship wherever it goes.

Pack Creativity

Reflecting the Maker

Because God is the Maker, creativity can help children notice beauty and express what is inside them.

Pack Packing Ideas

These are suggestions, not a required or complete list. Choose OCC-compliant items that fit your child, budget, and shoebox.

  • crayons or markers
  • dry watercolor set
  • stickers or craft kit

Talk Talk About It

Creative supplies invite a child to make something, not only receive something. That matters because God made a world full of color, order, variety, and beauty. We do not force a spiritual comparison onto crayons or paint. Instead, we notice that humans create because we are made by a creative God. Packing art supplies can help your family talk about beauty, patience, imagination, and the goodness of using our abilities for love.

For younger children:Let your child draw a tiny picture or choose colors for the box note.

Go deeper:Talk about how creativity can serve others: encouragement cards, songs, repairs, design, storytelling, hospitality.

Read Read God's Word

The heavens declare the glory of God, and the sky above proclaims his handiwork.

Psalm 19:1Creation itself teaches us to notice God's glory and beauty.

He has filled them with skill to do every sort of work...

Exodus 35:35God-given skill and craftsmanship can serve worship and community.

Ask Ask Your Child

  1. What beautiful thing has God made that you love to look at?
  2. How can making something become a way to love another person?
  3. What would you draw for the child receiving this box?

Connect Think About the Child Receiving This

These supplies give the recipient room to make something of their own. That is a way to honor their imagination.

Pray Pray Together

Lord, thank You for beauty and imagination. Bless the child who uses these supplies, and help them know the joy of making something good.

Pack School Tools

Growing in Wisdom

God values wisdom, and we can give tools that help another child learn and grow.

Pack Packing Ideas

These are suggestions, not a required or complete list. Choose OCC-compliant items that fit your child, budget, and shoebox.

  • notebook
  • ruler
  • calculator

Talk Talk About It

School supplies are practical, but they can also open a conversation about wisdom. The Bible does not treat wisdom as simply knowing facts; wisdom begins with reverence for the Lord and grows into a life that listens, learns, and acts well. A notebook or ruler is not magic. It is a useful tool that may help a child practice learning, organize thoughts, or participate in school or home activities.

For younger children:Show your child how the ruler or notebook works and ask what they like learning about.

Go deeper:Discuss the difference between knowledge and wisdom. How can learning become a way to love God and neighbor?

Read Read God's Word

The fear of the Lord is the beginning of knowledge; fools despise wisdom and instruction.

Proverbs 1:7Biblical wisdom begins with honoring God.

And Jesus increased in wisdom and in stature and in favor with God and man.

Luke 2:52Jesus' own childhood included real growth.

Ask Ask Your Child

  1. What is something you are learning that took practice?
  2. How can learning help us serve people?
  3. Why do you think God cares about wisdom?

Connect Think About the Child Receiving This

We should not assume what school is like for this child. We can simply give useful tools and pray they are a blessing in daily life.

Pray Pray Together

God of wisdom, help the child who receives these supplies grow in understanding. Teach our family to love learning and use knowledge to serve others.

Pack Writing Supplies

Words That Build Up

Words can give life, and Christians should use them to encourage and bless.

Pack Packing Ideas

These are suggestions, not a required or complete list. Choose OCC-compliant items that fit your child, budget, and shoebox.

  • pencils with sharpener
  • pens
  • erasers

Talk Talk About It

Writing tools help people record, practice, draw, solve, remember, and communicate. Scripture takes words seriously because words can wound or heal. As you pack pencils, pens, and erasers, talk about the words your family uses at home. Are they patient? truthful? encouraging? The child receiving these tools may use them in many ways, and your family can pray that their words and work become fruitful.

For younger children:Practice saying one encouraging sentence to each person in the room.

Go deeper:Talk about how written words can travel farther than spoken ones: notes, Scripture, letters, schoolwork, stories.

Read Read God's Word

Gracious words are like a honeycomb, sweetness to the soul and health to the body.

Proverbs 16:24Wise speech can strengthen another person.

Let no corrupting talk come out of your mouths, but only such as is good for building up, as fits the occasion, that it may give grace to those who hear.

Ephesians 4:29The New Testament calls believers to words that build up.

Ask Ask Your Child

  1. When has someone's words encouraged you?
  2. What kinds of words make a home feel safe and loving?
  3. What could you write in the note for this shoebox?

Connect Think About the Child Receiving This

The recipient may use these tools for school, drawing, lists, letters, or practice. Pray for words that bring courage and truth.

Pray Pray Together

Lord, guard our words and make them kind and true. Bless these writing tools, and let them be useful in the hands of the child who receives them.

Pack A Book or Picture Book

Listening to Truth

God speaks truthfully, and we learn to listen with humble hearts.

Pack Packing Ideas

These are suggestions, not a required or complete list. Choose OCC-compliant items that fit your child, budget, and shoebox.

  • picture book
  • wordless activity book
  • small notebook with simple drawings

Talk Talk About It

Books can invite attention, wonder, and repeated discovery. Choose something visually clear and culturally considerate, especially if language may be a barrier. This is also a moment to talk about Scripture: Christians believe God has spoken truly in His Word. We read the Bible not as a decoration for our lives but as light for our path. A simple book in the box can remind your family to be listeners and learners before God.

For younger children:Look through the pictures together and ask your child what story they notice.

Go deeper:Discuss why Christians test our ideas by Scripture instead of only by feelings, trends, or opinions.

Read Read God's Word

Your word is a lamp to my feet and a light to my path.

Psalm 119:105God's Word guides His people in real life.

All Scripture is breathed out by God and profitable for teaching, for reproof, for correction, and for training in righteousness,

2 Timothy 3:16Scripture forms what Christians believe and how we live.

Ask Ask Your Child

  1. What helps you listen well?
  2. Why do we need God's Word more than good advice alone?
  3. How can a picture or story help someone feel seen?

Connect Think About the Child Receiving This

We do not know the recipient's language, so choose a book with clear pictures or simple content that can still be enjoyed.

Pray Pray Together

God, thank You for speaking through Your Word. Help our family listen to You, and bless the child who receives this book with curiosity and joy.

Pack Personal Care

Bodies Matter

Because God made our bodies, ordinary care can be a way to honor Him and love our neighbor.

Pack Packing Ideas

These are suggestions, not a required or complete list. Choose OCC-compliant items that fit your child, budget, and shoebox.

  • toothbrush
  • comb or brush
  • washcloth

Talk Talk About It

Personal care items should be discussed with dignity. Do not say or imply that the recipient is dirty or neglected. Instead, talk about how God made people as whole persons: body and soul. Christians do not treat bodies as unimportant. Suggested items such as a toothbrush, comb, or washcloth can be useful in ordinary life, and packing this kind of gift gives your family a chance to thank God for daily care you may often overlook.

For younger children:Name a few everyday things your family uses and thank God for them.

Go deeper:Talk about why dignity matters when helping people. How can help become harmful when it sounds superior?

Read Read God's Word

Or do you not know that your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit within you, whom you have from God? You are not your own, for you were bought with a price. So glorify God in your body.

1 Corinthians 6:19-20Paul reminds believers that bodies belong to God.

...You shall love your neighbor as yourself.

Matthew 22:39Neighbor-love includes practical care without pride.

Ask Ask Your Child

  1. What daily care are you thankful for today?
  2. How can we help someone without talking down to them?
  3. Why do bodies matter to God?

Connect Think About the Child Receiving This

Pack these items as useful gifts for a child made and loved by God, not as a judgment about that child's life.

Pray Pray Together

Lord, thank You for making us body and soul. Bless these useful gifts, and teach us to care for people with humility and respect.

Pack Cup or Eating Utensils

Daily Bread

Everyday needs can teach us gratitude for God's provision.

Pack Packing Ideas

These are suggestions, not a required or complete list. Choose OCC-compliant items that fit your child, budget, and shoebox.

  • sturdy cup
  • spoon and fork set
  • small bowl

Talk Talk About It

OCC recommends eating utensils among personal care items. Because food itself is prohibited, this is a good place to talk about provision without packing food. Jesus taught His disciples to ask the Father for daily bread. That prayer trains us to depend on God, give thanks, and notice people around us. An idea such as a cup or utensil is humble, useful, and easy to overlook, which makes it a strong family conversation about gratitude.

For younger children:Hold the cup and name something God provided today.

Go deeper:Discuss the difference between wants and needs. How can abundance make gratitude harder?

Read Read God's Word

Give us this day our daily bread

Matthew 6:11Jesus teaches daily dependence on the Father.

Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above...

James 1:17Gratitude looks beyond the gift to the Giver.

Ask Ask Your Child

  1. What did God provide for us today that we almost forgot to notice?
  2. Why does Jesus teach us to ask for daily bread?
  3. How can gratitude make us more generous?

Connect Think About the Child Receiving This

We do not know how this item will be used, but we can pray it becomes a helpful part of ordinary days.

Pray Pray Together

Father, give us grateful hearts. Please provide for the child and family connected to this box, and teach us to trust You day by day.

Pack Clothing

Compassion in Action

Love becomes visible when we notice practical needs and respond with compassion.

Pack Packing Ideas

These are suggestions, not a required or complete list. Choose OCC-compliant items that fit your child, budget, and shoebox.

  • shirt
  • socks or undergarments
  • hat

Talk Talk About It

Clothing is one of the most practical gifts in a shoebox. Scripture often connects love for God with concrete mercy toward people. This does not mean we know the recipient lacks clothing. It means we know bodies need care, seasons change, and practical love matters. Let this moment slow your family down enough to ask: Are we willing to love in ways that are useful, not only impressive?

For younger children:Let your child feel the fabric and talk about choosing something comfortable and durable.

Go deeper:Talk about Matthew 25 carefully: Jesus identifies with people in need, and His followers cannot separate faith from compassion.

Read Read God's Word

If a brother or sister is poorly clothed and lacking in daily food, and one of you says to them, “Go in peace, be warmed and filled,” without giving them the things needed for the body, what good is that?

James 2:15-16James connects genuine faith with practical care for bodily needs.

I was naked and you clothed me, I was sick and you visited me...

Matthew 25:36Jesus teaches that practical compassion matters deeply to Him.

Ask Ask Your Child

  1. What makes clothing comfortable, useful, and respectful?
  2. Why does God care about practical compassion?
  3. What is one practical way our family can love someone nearby this week?

Connect Think About the Child Receiving This

Choose clothing that is new, sturdy, and appropriate. Avoid messages or images that could be confusing across cultures.

Pray Pray Together

Jesus, make our compassion practical. Bless this clothing, and teach our family to notice real needs with humble hearts.

Pack Shoes or Flip-Flops

Walking in Love

Those who receive God's love are sent to walk in love.

Pack Packing Ideas

These are suggestions, not a required or complete list. Choose OCC-compliant items that fit your child, budget, and shoebox.

  • flip-flops
  • canvas shoes
  • sandals

Talk Talk About It

Shoes are useful, but do not turn them into a forced lesson about the recipient's circumstances. Instead, let them start a conversation about walking with God. The Bible often describes life as a path: we can walk humbly, walk in wisdom, walk in love. OCC also reminds families that shoeboxes move outward through many hands: packers, volunteers, churches, ministry partners. God sends His people into the world as witnesses of His love.

For younger children:Take a few steps together and say, "Lord, help us walk in love."

Go deeper:Discuss how Christian mission is not only traveling far away; it is faithful witness wherever God places us.

Read Read God's Word

For so the Lord has commanded us, saying, “‘I have made you a light for the Gentiles, that you may bring salvation to the ends of the earth.’”

Acts 13:47God calls His people to be His hands and feet to carry His word everywhere..

And how are they to preach unless they are sent? As it is written, “How beautiful are the feet of those who preach the good news!”

Romans 10:15This connects feet, going, and the beauty of carrying good news.

Ask Ask Your Child

  1. What does it mean to walk humbly with God?
  2. Where is God asking our family to walk in love?
  3. Who are some people involved in this shoebox's journey after it leaves us?

Connect Think About the Child Receiving This

The child may use these in ways we cannot predict. Pray for every step in the shoebox journey and every person who handles it.

Pray Pray Together

Lord, guide our steps. Bless the child who receives these, and bless every volunteer and local believer serving along the way.

Pack Useful Extras

Serving with Care

Serving well means paying attention to small details that can help someone else.

Pack Packing Ideas

These are suggestions, not a required or complete list. Choose OCC-compliant items that fit your child, budget, and shoebox.

  • flashlight with extra batteries outside the device
  • hair accessories
  • small backpack or drawstring bag

Talk Talk About It

Some gifts are not dramatic, but they are thoughtful. A flashlight, bag, or hair accessory can be genuinely useful. Service often looks like that: quiet attention rather than applause. Jesus washed His disciples' feet, taking the place of a servant. He did not love in theory. He loved with a towel in His hands. This category invites your family to ask whether you are willing to serve in hidden, practical ways.

For younger children:Ask, "What little thing helps you during the day?"

Go deeper:Talk about hidden service. Why do we sometimes prefer impressive acts over faithful small ones?

Read Read God's Word

If I then, your Lord and Teacher, have washed your feet, you also ought to wash one another's feet. For I have given you an example, that you also should do just as I have done to you.

John 13:14-15Jesus gives His disciples a pattern of humble service.

For you were called to freedom, brothers. Only do not use your freedom as an opportunity for the flesh, but through love serve one another.

Galatians 5:13Christian freedom expresses itself in loving service.

Ask Ask Your Child

  1. What small helpful thing has someone done for you?
  2. Why does Jesus connect greatness with serving?
  3. What is one quiet way you can serve at home this week?

Connect Think About the Child Receiving This

Small practical items can communicate care. Choose them thoughtfully and check that they meet OCC rules.

Pray Pray Together

Jesus, You served with humility. Make us attentive to small needs, and use these practical gifts to bless the child who receives them.

Pack A Personal Note and Photo

Sharing God's Love

The best gifts point beyond themselves to the love of God shown in Jesus Christ.

Pack Packing Ideas

These are suggestions, not a required or complete list. Choose OCC-compliant items that fit your child, budget, and shoebox.

  • short personal note
  • family photo
  • drawing from your child

Talk Talk About It

OCC says a note and photo are often favorite items. This final piece makes the box personal without making it official or complicated. Keep the note warm, simple, and appropriate: first names, a greeting, something your family enjoys, and a sentence about praying for the child. This is also where the journey's deepest truth comes together: Christians give because God first loved us. OCC's stated purpose is not only to send items, but to create opportunities through local churches for children to hear the Good News of Jesus Christ and, where offered, be invited into discipleship through The Greatest Journey.

For younger children:Let your child draw a picture or dictate one kind sentence.

Go deeper:Talk about witness. How can we speak truthfully about Jesus with gentleness, respect, and love?

Read Read God's Word

We love because he first loved us.

1 John 4:19Christian love is a response to God's prior love.

Go therefore and make disciples of all nations,,,

Matthew 28:19Jesus sends His followers into disciple-making mission.

Ask Ask Your Child

  1. What do you want the child receiving this box to know?
  2. How can we talk about Jesus with kindness and truth?
  3. What have we learned about why Christians give?

Connect Think About the Child Receiving This

The recipient is not anonymous to God. Your note can be a small sign that a real family prayed and packed with care.

Pray Pray Together

Lord Jesus, thank You for loving us first. Use this box, the local church, and every Gospel opportunity connected to it for Your glory and this child's good. Amen.

Finishing experience

Your shoebox is packed. You're not quite finished.

Look Back

Ask:

  • What do you remember most?
  • What did we learn about God?
  • What changed about how we think of the child who may receive this box?

Write a Note

If you have not already done so, include a warm note, drawing, or photo. Keep it simple, respectful, and appropriate.

Pray Over the Box

Lord Jesus, thank You for loving us first. We ask You to bless the child who receives this shoebox, their family, the volunteers, the local church or ministry partner, and every Gospel opportunity connected to this gift. Use this box in a way that honors You. Amen.

Follow Official Instructions

Use current OCC resources for labeling, the suggested donation, Follow Your Box, drop-off details, and final prohibited-item checks.

What happens next?

Your child may ask, "What happens next?"

After shoeboxes are packed:

  1. Families drop them off at their church or a drop off location.
  2. From there the boxes are moved to an area wide drop off location where they are packed onto vehicles.
  3. The boxes are then taken to a regional processing center. In York, that is Baltimore.
  4. The processing center has an army of volunteers who inspect the boxes to ensure there are no inapproriate items and repair any damages boxes. The boxes are then packed into cartons for loaded onto shipping containers.
  5. The shipping containers are shipped internationally to areas where local ministry partners are requesting them.
  6. The cartons are opened and the boxes are carried, sometimes a long way on foot to be delivered to the children at child-friendly outreach events.
  7. Many children hear the Good News of Jesus Christ and, where offered, are invited to The Greatest Journey follow-up discipleship program.
